## Direct answers

**Q1 — Why did LV15 beat H028 in 2015?**
- **LV15 was better almost all year, in both rising and falling months.**
  - H028 was behind LV15 in 10 of 12 months.
  - The worst month (July, −3.4%) is only 27% of the year's gap; the worst three months make up 53%.
  - About 60% of the gap came in SPY-up months and 40% in SPY-down months.
- **LV15 simply had the better profile that year.** It returned +9.5% while SPY made +0.7%.
  - It fell much less in down months (down-capture 0.31 against H028's 0.60).
  - It still participated more in up months (up-capture 0.79 against 0.39).
- H028 had more volatility (14.8% against 11.3%) and a deeper drawdown (−11.8% against −7.3%), with negative return per unit of risk.
- **2015 was a strong year for low-volatility stocks generally.** The lowest-volatility quintile of the eligible universe beat the universe average by about +4.6 points over the year.

**Q2 — Why did LV15 beat H028 in 2016?**
- **Mostly in a few down months, above all October 2016.**
  - In the 8 SPY-up months H028 was slightly *ahead* of LV15 (+0.6% summed).
  - In the 4 SPY-down months it lost 5.8%. October alone was −3.0%, 58% of the year's net gap.
- LV15 hardly fell when SPY fell (down-capture 0.07); H028 fell almost as much as SPY (down-capture 0.90).
- This was **not a broad low-volatility tailwind.** The lowest-volatility quintile *underperformed* the universe in 2016 (about −2.2 points), and LV15 itself only matched SPY.
- λ was already 100 in 2016, so λ selection cannot explain it.

**Q3 — Was it the same reason in both years?**
**No.**
- 2015 was a broad shortfall, every month and in both market directions. It came against a strong low-volatility year, with an aggressive model that λ = 100 then shrank.
- 2016 was a narrow shortfall concentrated in down months (one month above all), with a model already at λ = 100. H028 kept pace with LV15 when the market rose.

By the pre-registered rules:
- 2015 = "broad" (B);
- 2016 = "mixed" (E): 7 of 12 months behind, but the worst three months exceed the whole net gap;
- the dominant market side differs (2015: SPY-up months; 2016: SPY-down months).

That gives **C**.

**Q4 — Did fixing λ = 100 solve most of the 2015 problem?**
**No, about a quarter of it.**
- The 2015 gap to LV15 shrank from −0.171 to −0.126 (log). Over 2015–2016 combined, H027's gap of −0.230 became −0.178.
- **What shrinkage fixed:**
  - the downside: down-capture 1.01 → 0.60;
  - MaxDD −15.9% → −11.8%;
  - volatility 15.8% → 14.8%.
- **What it did not fix:** participation in rising months. Up-capture fell further, 0.55 → 0.39, far below LV15's 0.79. Turnover rose (8.1 → 10.0).

**Q5 — Useful but regime-dependent, or concentrated luck?**
**Descriptively, the record against LV15 looks period-dependent rather than one or two lucky observations. But the evidence is weak and already seen.**
- **Period-dependent pattern:**
  - H028 lost to LV15 in 2015 (broad) and 2016 (concentrated);
  - it then beat LV15 in every year 2017–2021, and in 2017 in 9 of 12 months;
  - March 2020 is only 5% of H028's lead over LV15 (P13-CP1), so the lead is not one crisis month.
- **The 2016/2017 contrast follows market direction:**
  - essentially the same model (coefficient cosine similarity 0.91);
  - lost in a year with four down months (2016);
  - won in a year with none (2017; up-capture 1.41 against LV15's 0.53).

  That is consistent with a higher-beta tilt than LV15's (2016 beta 0.68 against 0.50) whose results depend on the market path.
- **What this does not show:** seven yearly observations cannot separate "regime-dependent information" from luck. The conditional-null p-value (0.0004) is development evidence on fully seen data. **No status is upgraded; H028 remains REJECTED.**
