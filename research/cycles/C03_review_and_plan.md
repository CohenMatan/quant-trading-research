## 1. What we learned (one page)

1. **The strategies lacked edge; the method did not hide it.**
   - Across all 37 variations the median in-sample Sharpe was 0.50 net of costs and 0.81 before costs. The equal-weight ≥ $2B benchmark had 0.92 over the same dates.
   - A more precise measure is the information ratio (IR): the return a strategy earns beyond what its exposure to the benchmark explains, divided by the risk of that extra return.
   - The median variation's net IR was **−0.14**, and **+0.12** before costs. The screen's "Sharpe ≥ EW + 0.10" item requires a net IR of about **0.5** (§2.7).
   - Only 2 of 37 cleared that bar: H005 (low volatility; net IR 0.8–1.1) and H007 v1.1 (squeeze; 0.88). Both are defensive, mostly-cash profiles, and both failed a later test (Validation and robustness respectively).
2. **Costs matter, but they are not the main reason for failure.**
   - Commissions plus slippage cost 0.5–14% of equity a year (median 2.6%), which is roughly 0.25 of IR.
   - Before costs, 30 of 37 would still fail the Sharpe-vs-benchmark item. Short-horizon ideas (H001, H004) were destroyed by costs of 7–14% a year.
3. **Most variations were the same bet.**
   - Average return correlation with the benchmark was 0.73 and average beta 0.69.
   - A single common factor explains 61% of the variance of all 37 variations' daily returns.
   - Distinct hypotheses were still mostly "long about 10–15 large US stocks, about 80–90% invested", with small tilts.
4. **Defensive, low-exposure profiles are the only ones that looked good, and they are fragile.**
   - H005 and E007-02 had high Sharpe ratios because they were in cash or in quiet stocks for long stretches.
   - Both were tilted towards takeover targets: up to 18% (H005 v1.0 and v1.2) and 13.5% (E007-02) of their trades ended in takeover cash-outs.
   - E007-02's result sat on a narrow parameter peak.
5. **The protocol worked as intended.**
   - Pre-declared variations, frozen gates, the D069 trial count, the frozen cycle-level PBO (D073), robustness batteries and verification canaries caught one strategy defect before it ran (H008) and several infrastructure defects (D057, D059, D063, D072).
   - No result was rescued after the fact.

## 2. Review of the research method (the owner's seven questions)

For each question: the evidence, then whether it points to a **strategy weakness** or a **limitation of our method**.

### 2.1 Do portfolio construction and position sizing unnecessarily limit performance?

- **Rules:** 10 slots (C02) or 15 (C01); at most 10% per position; at least $5,000 per position; 2% cash buffer; buys funded only by cash already held, with a 15% reserve for opening gaps (D051).
- **Evidence:**
  - C02 event strategies were 77–91% invested whenever signals existed. The binding limits were signal scarcity (E007-02, 21%) and, in C01's monthly strategies, rebalancing timing (48–83%).
  - Because Sharpe does not change with the fraction invested (cash earns 0 in our model), under-investment lowers returns but hardly changes Sharpe. The exposure-matched comparisons confirm it: most variations also lose to the benchmark held at their own exposure.
- **Concentration is a real handicap.** A 10-stock book carries idiosyncratic risk that the 1,000-stock benchmark does not. That risk lowers the Sharpe of a no-skill 10-stock portfolio below the benchmark's; a first estimate is roughly 0.8 against 0.92. It is not yet measured on our data (M1).
- **Verdict:** a **partial method limitation.** Concentration makes the Sharpe-vs-benchmark comparison harder for a skill-less book, but it cannot turn negative IRs into positive ones. Worth measuring (M1, M2); not worth relaxing.

### 2.2 Does the execution model create excessive idle cash?

- The 2% buffer plus the 15% gap reserve leaves about 9–10% of equity structurally in cash for fully-invested strategies (average exposure 0.89–0.91).
- Measured as benchmark return forgone, idle cash cost 1–2% a year for C02 event strategies, and up to 5–11% a year for low-exposure ones. Sharpe is almost unaffected.
- **Verdict:** a small, Sharpe-neutral method cost; **not a cause of failure.** The 15% gap reserve was set after real gaps of 5.5–12.9% (D051). Reducing it would need evidence on actual opening gaps; it is optional (M3).

### 2.3 How much do commissions and slippage matter?

| Group | Round trips per year | Costs, % of equity per year | Net → gross Sharpe (median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short-horizon (H001, H004) | 175–441 | 4.1–14% | 0.03 → 0.64 |
| Monthly (H002, H003, H005, H008, H011) | 21–172 | 0.5–4.5% | 0.62 → 0.71 |
| Event, multi-week (H006, H007, H009, H010) | 28–118 | 0.9–3.5% | 0.71 → 0.88 |

- About half of costs is the 10 bps slippage per side (54%), and half the $7 commission (46%).
- **Verdict:** costs are **decisive for short horizons** (a genuine weakness of those ideas at realistic costs). They are **material but not decisive** for multi-week ideas. The cost model stays: 10 bps per side for ≥ $2B stocks is realistic, not conservative.

### 2.4 Does the $100K account size affect viability?

- The $7 commission is 0.07% per side on a $9.8K position; it would be 0.007% at $1M. Commission drag was 0.2–8% a year; at 10× the account size it would be about a tenth of that.
- Slippage (proportional) does not change with account size.
- **The larger effect is indirect.** The $5,000 minimum position caps the book at about 19 positions (about 15 in practice with the buffers). Broadly diversified designs, which are the natural way to harvest small, broad edges, are therefore impossible at $100K.
- **Verdict:** a **method constraint, set by the owner's real account** (D041, D044). It excludes a whole class of designs, and that is legitimate if the product must run at $100K. Measure the effect (M2) before deciding anything.

### 2.5 Are the universe rules appropriate?

- US common stocks, point-in-time market cap ≥ $2B, price ≥ $5, 20-day average dollar volume ≥ $5M: about 700–1,300 stocks a day in 2010–2017. That is liquid and tradeable with realistic slippage.
- The D057 fixes removed funds and partnerships.
- One side effect: low-volatility and squeeze screens are drawn to **takeover targets** (flat prices after a bid). That comes from the strategies, not the universe, and the rules may not filter it (owner clarification 2).
- **Verdict:** **appropriate, with no change recommended.** The takeover diagnostic should stay prominent.

### 2.6 Are the strategies distinct enough?

- Correlation between hypotheses averaged 0.58 (range 0.18–0.85); within a hypothesis, 0.75.
- The common factor (61% of variance) is essentially "long the large-cap market". Only E007-02 and H005 were materially different, and both by holding cash.
- **Verdict:** a **genuine weakness of the idea set.** Stock-selection tilts on a concentrated, fully-invested long-only book mostly re-express market beta. New ideas should differ in *what drives returns*, not only in the entry signal.

### 2.7 Is the screen aligned with the objective?

- For a long-only book with correlation ρ to the benchmark, Sharpe ≈ ρ × benchmark Sharpe + √(1 − ρ²) × IR.
- At our typical ρ of about 0.75 and benchmark Sharpe 0.92, "Sharpe ≥ benchmark + 0.10" requires a net IR of about 0.5: about 1.4 standard errors of skill over 8 years.
- That is demanding but consistent with the objective: a strategy that cannot beat simply holding the universe by a margin is not worth trading.
- The other screen items (≥ 100 trades, drawdown no worse than the benchmark, stress episodes, profit factor, expectancy tests, year concentration) test robustness of the edge and are aligned.
- **Verdict:** **aligned; no relaxation recommended.**
  - Diagnostic to add: a matched no-skill null (M1). It separates "this design has a structural handicap" from "this signal has no edge".
  - The Deflated Sharpe bar rises with every trial. With the current Sharpe dispersion, the best of N skill-less strategies is expected to show an annual Sharpe of 1.08 at N = 37, 1.14 at N = 49 and 1.18 at N = 61. More hypotheses make the final gate harder, so C03 should test **fewer, better** ideas.

### Summary: strategy weaknesses vs method limitations

| Finding | Type |
|---|---|
| Median net IR −0.14; almost no variation earns a benchmark-beating edge even before costs | Strategy weakness |
| Short-horizon ideas killed by realistic costs | Strategy weakness (at our costs) |
| Ideas mostly re-express market beta (61% common factor) | Strategy weakness (idea design) |
| Defensive profiles fragile and takeover-tilted | Strategy weakness |
| 10–15-stock concentration lowers no-skill Sharpe below the benchmark | Method limitation; size unmeasured (M1) |
| $5K minimum at $100K rules out diversified designs | Method constraint set by the account (M2) |
| About 9–10% structural cash from the buffer and gap reserve | Small method cost, Sharpe-neutral (M3, optional) |
| Screen and gates | Aligned; keep |

## 3. Recommended methodological investigations (none is implemented)

All three are verification or measurement, not trials. None changes a gate. Each needs owner approval before it runs.

- **M1 — Matched no-skill null.** A canary strategy that picks *random* eligible stocks with the same slots, holding-period distribution, turnover, costs and cash rules as a given design. Its seed is fixed, and it is run for several seeds.
  - It measures the Sharpe a no-skill version of each design achieves in IS: the structural handicap from concentration, costs and cash.
  - **Use:** reported next to every C03 result, as "net IR over the matched null". **Gates unchanged.**
  - **Cost:** about 6 runs (3 designs × 2 seeds) at about 20 minutes each.
- **M2 — Breadth and account size.** Run the same null at 10, 15 and 19 slots at $100K, and at 30 slots at $250K (kind `sizing`, D044).
  - This quantifies how much concentration costs no-skill Sharpe, and whether a larger account would change what is testable.
  - **Decision for the owner afterwards:** keep $100K as the only target, or accept a larger research account for diversified designs. No change is proposed now.
  - **Cost:** about 4 runs.
- **M3 (optional) — Gap reserve evidence.** Measure actual opening gaps on our buy orders from C01/C02 fills, then propose, or not, a smaller reserve. It is expected to be Sharpe-neutral, so it is low priority.

## 4. Candidate strategy families for C03

**Selection principles, from §1–2:**

- Ideas whose returns are *not* just market beta.
- Low turnover (costs).
- Pre-2018 literature only, and no motivation from post-2017 events.
- Price and volume only.
- Distinct from all 37 tested variations.

Proposed shortlist: **3 hypotheses**, so N rises from 37 to 46 (DSR hurdle about 1.13).

| # | Family | Idea (pre-2018 literature) | Why it is new | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Calendar timing: turn of the month** | Hold a fixed diversified large-cap basket only from the last trading day of each month to the third trading day of the next; otherwise hold cash (Ariel 1987; Lakonishok & Smidt 1988; McConnell & Xu 2008) | Timing, not stock selection; about 25% exposure; returns from a recurring flow effect, not beta | Low exposure; must be judged exposure-aware; the effect may have weakened after publication |
| B | **Volatility-managed exposure** | Scale the size of a diversified basket inversely to its recent realised volatility (Moreira & Muir 2017; Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015 for momentum) | Changes *how much* is held, not *what*; attacks the drawdown and beta that sank C01/C02 | The benefit is mostly in crashes; IS 2010–2017 has few, so the IS test may be uninformative |
| C | **Lottery avoidance (MAX)** | Hold the universe *minus* stocks with extreme recent maximum daily returns (Bali, Cakici & Whitelaw 2011) | An exclusion tilt on a broad basket, not a concentrated pick | Needs breadth; at $100K only about 15–19 names, so M2 matters |

**Considered and not proposed:**

- Industry momentum (Moskowitz & Grinblatt 1999): it needs industry classifications, and Morningstar's are current, not point-in-time (D061 risk). It is also not purely price/volume.
- Low turnover or liquidity premia: close to H005's defensive profile.
- More short-horizon reversal: killed by costs (H001/H004).
- Idiosyncratic volatility: close to H005, which failed Validation.

**Note:** A and B need a "diversified basket" to time or scale. Within the universe rules that means about 15–19 equal-weighted largest stocks, re-selected monthly by market cap. SPY itself is an ETF and outside the "US common stocks" rule. This is an owner decision (§8).

## 7. Infrastructure and data needs

- **Matched-null strategy (M1/M2):** a small canary template with a fixed seed, reusing the harness. Unit tests: seeded determinism, identical cash and cost rules.
- **Basket selection (A, B):** the largest-N eligible stocks, re-selected monthly, using point-in-time market cap already in the harness. The calendar helper already exists (`qr_is_last_session_of_month`); a "first K sessions of the month" helper is new.
- **Exposure scaling (B):** target weights below 100%, which `qr_rebalance` already supports. Realised volatility comes from existing windows.
- **MAX (C):** from the existing daily close windows.
- **No new data source and no purchase.**
- **Operational:** keep D077 recovery. Consider adding an automatic "resume queue" wrapper, because container restarts cost several hours in C02.

## 8. Controlling overfitting and the cumulative trial count

1. **Fewer hypotheses.** Three, each with three pre-declared variations. N goes from 37 to 46; the DSR bar is about 1.13 and is stated in advance.
2. **Everything pre-declared and frozen** before any C03 result: hypotheses, variations, perturbations, the null design, the budget cap. D069, D073 and the D036 gates are unchanged.
3. **The nulls are not trials.** They are infrastructure measurements: no selection, no tuning, and their results are reported before C03 selection runs.
4. **No reuse of rejected ideas.** Nothing from H001–H011 is re-tested under a new name. Families A–C are checked against the 37 variations for correlation *after* the runs, and reported.
5. **Hindsight rule.** A–C come from literature published by 2017 and are not motivated by 2018–2026 events. The 2018–2021 Validation period is untouched for design.
6. **Stopping rule.** If C03 also ends with no candidate, recommend closing Phase 1 with a final report rather than a C04, because the DSR bar keeps rising.
