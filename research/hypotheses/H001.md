# H001 — Short-term reversal inside a long-term uptrend

- **Status:** registered 2026-09-28, before any C01 data was examined.
- **Cycle:** C01.
- **Strategy:** S001.

**Hypothesis.** Large, liquid US stocks in a long-term uptrend that suffer a sharp decline over a few days tend to partially rebound over the following one to two weeks.

**Mechanism.**

- Short-term price pressure from liquidity-demanding sellers is later absorbed by liquidity providers. Evidence: Lehmann (1990), Jegadeesh (1990), Nagel (2012, "Evaporating liquidity").
- Requiring a long-term uptrend (price above its 200-day average) screens out stocks whose declines are more likely to reflect deteriorating fundamentals.

**Prediction (IS 2010–2017).**

- The entries outperform the equal-weight ≥ $2B universe after $7/order commissions and 10 bps slippage.
- Positive expectancy per trade, with most profit arriving within 10 trading days.

**Risks and why it may fail.**

- The edge per trade is small relative to the cost of about 0.42% per round trip at a $6.5K position.
- The effect has weakened for large caps as market-making improved.
- Crashes cluster losses.

**Disclosure.** The CP2 pipeline demo (S000, a 5-day reversal without a trend filter, 2010–14) was seen before this hypothesis. It lost most of its gross edge to costs. This hypothesis differs by the trend filter, the oversold measure and the exits, but it is **not independent** of that observation.
