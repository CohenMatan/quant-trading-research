## Hypothesis

Among US common stocks of at least $2B market cap (financials and REITs excluded for H032), companies with positive operating cash flow and positive revenue growth in their latest fiscal year that reduced their share count the most over the last twelve months outperform SPY after realistic costs.

## Why it might be true

- Net share repurchases are among the best-documented equity anomalies (Ikenberry, Lakonishok & Vermaelen 1995; Pontiff & Woodgate 2008; Daniel & Titman 2006 on net issuance).
- Buybacks by companies that generate operating cash and are not shrinking are a more credible signal of management's view than buybacks by cash-burning or declining businesses (Jensen 1986; Lie 2005; Hribar, Jenkins & Johnson 2006).

## Why it might be false

- The repurchase premium has weakened since publication, and in large caps.
- The filters may remove the value / bad-news buybacks that carried much of the historical premium.
- A 15-stock book over about ten years has wide tracking error: a +2% a year edge is hard to detect.
- The 2010s favoured growth and technology; the filters tilt toward them (OS-CP1 §11.5).

## Caveats (owner D278)

- Positive operating cash flow does not prove that repurchases were funded from operations.
- Positive revenue growth does not establish future outperformance.
- Beating random books drawn from the filtered pool supports the ranking hypothesis; it does not show that the filters improve the original strategy. B0 (unfiltered) is never promoted if B1 fails.
