## Hypothesis

A small, interpretable set of point-in-time features from the frozen approved data predicts next-month cross-sectional total shareholder return over 2011–2017. The set has 2–4 features, equal-weighted within-date percentiles, drawn from a frozen 11-feature library of momentum, risk, profitability, accruals, valuation, investment, reversal and leverage measures.

The prediction must be:
- significant after correcting for the whole feature and model search;
- economically meaningful (80+ group);
- monotone;
- stable;
- not dependent on one feature;
- inside a non-dead neighbourhood of related models.

Such a result would justify one untouched validation on 2018–2021.

## Why it might be true

Score v1 forced fixed quintile bands and fixed layer weights onto features of uneven quality. It included a sector-breadth state and a trend state with little academic support, and revenue growth with an ambiguous direction.

The redesigned library keeps the best-documented anomalies (momentum, low risk, profitability, accruals) and adds value, asset growth, short-term reversal, cash profitability, 52-week-high proximity and cash-flow yield. All are point-in-time from the frozen data.

## Why it might be false

- Score v1 and every reweighting were null.
- 12-1 momentum did not reproduce in this universe for 2011–2017 (H019).
- GP/A alone failed (H016).
- Large-cap US anomalies have weakened after publication.
- 415 models give a large search, and the null calibration will demand a high bar.
