# H012: Volatility-managed exposure

- **Status:** APPROVED 2026-09-30 for C03 (`docs/checkpoints/CP3e_C03_final_plan.md`; methodology D082). Implementation: S012 (in progress); not run.
- **Family:** exposure timing, not stock selection. **Strategy:** S012.

## Rationale

- Stock-market volatility is persistent: high recent volatility predicts high near-term volatility.
- Expected returns do not rise in proportion, so the reward per unit of risk is low when volatility is high.
- Scaling exposure down in high-volatility regimes (and back up in calm ones) should therefore raise risk-adjusted returns.

**Literature (pre-2018):**

- Fleming, Kirby & Ostdiek (2001), *J. Finance*;
- Barroso & Santa-Clara (2015), *JFE*;
- Moreira & Muir (2017), *J. Finance*;
- volatility persistence: Engle (1982), Bollerslev (1986).

No post-2017 event motivates it, and it is not related to any Validation result.

## Rules (fixed before any C03 result)

**Indicator (SPY, never traded).**

- RV(n) = sample standard deviation of SPY's last n daily simple returns ending at the decision close T, × √252. Computed from split- and dividend-adjusted closes kept point in time by the harness.

**Traded basket.**

- The 15 largest eligible stocks by point-in-time market cap, from the same Morningstar MarketCap the universe filter uses on day T. Ties go by security ID.
- Reconstituted at the first session of January, April, July and October.
- Equal weight within the basket.

**Exposure.**

- At the last session of each month (v1.2: the last session of each week): target e = min(1, RV(252) / RV(s)), with s = 21 (v1.1: 63).
- Positions are resized to e × equal weight at the next open, only if |e − current| > 0.10 (the band).
- No leverage: e ≤ 1.

**Execution and costs.** Signal at the T close, orders at the T+1 open, $7 per order, 10 bps slippage, D051 cash rules.

## Controls (benchmarks, not trials)

- **Control A:** the same basket, the same reconstitution, **always e = 1**.
- **Control B (causal, exposure-matched):** the same basket and reconstitution. At each rescale date, e_B = the mean of the variation's own targets over the previous 12 rescale dates (the first year uses the available mean).
  - It holds about the same average exposure without responding to the current volatility regime.
  - Only past information is used.
  - One Control B per variation.
- **Descriptive only:** Control A scaled to the variation's realised mean exposure (a constant, known only afterwards). Used for drawdown and CAGR comparison, and labelled ex post.

## Falsification

- **Reject H012** if every variation fails the unchanged IS screen.
- **Timing value is refuted** if Sharpe(variation) − Sharpe(Control A) ≤ 0.05 **and** Sharpe(variation) − Sharpe(Control B) ≤ 0.05 for all variations.
  - With cash at 0%, a constant cash share cannot raise Sharpe, so the Sharpe differences measure timing alone.
- **A lower drawdown alone is never accepted as evidence of timing.** It must be lower than Control B's at a mean exposure within ±0.05.
