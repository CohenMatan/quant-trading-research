## Hypothesis

When at least two distinct directors or officers buy their own company's common stock in the open market — each at least $25,000, together at least $100,000 — and the purchases become public on EDGAR within ten calendar days, the stock beats SPY over the next 20 trading sessions.

The test enters at the next regular-session open after the SEC acceptance that completes the cluster and exits at the close of the 20th session. Large, liquid US stocks only (frozen Data v2 universe).

This is a new, publicly disclosed corporate event followed by a future return. It is not a cross-sectional characteristic score.

## Why it might be true

- Insiders spending personal capital on open-market purchases is a costly, voluntary signal. Several insiders doing so independently within days is harder to explain by liquidity or diversification needs than a single purchase.
- The academic literature documents abnormal returns after insider purchases, stronger for clusters and for purchases by several insiders. It is published before 2017 (e.g. Seyhun 1986; Lakonishok and Lee 2001; Jeng, Metrick and Zeckhauser 2003; Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski 2012 on opportunistic trades).
- The test uses only information public at the SEC acceptance timestamp.

## Why it might be false

- Most documented insider-purchase returns are concentrated in small and illiquid stocks. The ≥ $2B universe may hold too few clusters, or none with an edge.
- The effect may have been arbitraged away after EDGAR filings became machine-readable (2003 onward), and certainly by 2011–2017.
- Clusters often follow sharp price falls (insiders buying the dip). A 20-session window may capture continued weakness or plain mean reversion that the matched null also shows.

## Not motivated by post-2017 events

The definition follows the owner's D204 parameters and pre-2017 literature. No 2018+ market knowledge was used.
