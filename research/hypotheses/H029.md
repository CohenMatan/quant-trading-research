## Hypothesis

A transparent, equal-weight Business Improvement Score contains stock-selection information that is **independent** of the frozen H028 learned score. This information shows up particularly in **rising markets**. The score averages six oriented, comparable-period improvement signals:
- F1 revenue-growth acceleration;
- F2 gross-margin change;
- F4 operating-cash-flow / assets change;
- F5 ROA change;
- F7 net share issuance (inverted);
- F8 asset-turnover change.

## Why it might be true

- Improving fundamentals (accelerating sales, widening margins, rising cash generation and returns on assets, buybacks rather than dilution) are classic, economically motivated signals.
- They are slow to be priced, and they are unrelated by construction to H028's defensive (low-volatility-tilted) learned score.
- In P14-CP1 the six were largely non-redundant (max ρ 0.39) and distinct from H028's features (max 0.47).

## Why it might be false

- Fundamental-momentum effects documented before 2010 may have decayed in large US caps (≥ $2B) after publication.
- Quarterly fundamentals change slowly, so a monthly Top-15 portfolio may mostly hold large, already-priced names.
- Equal weights are not tuned; one or two families could dilute the others.
- Rising-market outperformance may be indistinguishable from random selection of the same score distribution.

## Motivation and its limits

- The family list and constructions come from the pre-registered P14-CP1 feasibility audit (data quality only) and owner D229.
- No post-2017 event motivates any rule.
- H029 is **not** tuned to H028's failure years (2015, 2016, 2018, 2020).
- No ML, no λ, no fitted weights, no feature selection, no interaction search.
