# H008: Residual (idiosyncratic) relative strength

- **Status:** proposed 2026-09-29 for C02. Approved by the owner 2026-09-29 with the C02 plan.
- **Family:** relative strength, ranking on stock-specific returns.
- **Strategy:** S008.

## Rationale

**Mechanism.**

- Ordinary momentum ranks on *total* return, so it loads on whatever factor has been winning, above all market beta.
- It therefore suffers "momentum crashes" when the market rebounds.
- Ranking on the part of the return the market does not explain (the residual) keeps the under-reaction to firm-specific news and reduces the factor bet in the *selection*. The long-only portfolio itself still carries market exposure.

**Literature (pre-2018).**

- Residual momentum: Grundy & Martin (2001); Gutierrez & Pirinsky (2007); Blitz, Huij & Martens (2011). The last shows residual momentum earns a similar premium to total-return momentum at about half the volatility.
- Momentum crashes: Daniel & Moskowitz (2016).

## Rules

*(Amended 2026-09-29, D074 option A, owner-approved before any run.)* The first version regressed and summed residuals over the same window. Least-squares residuals with an intercept sum to exactly zero there, so every score was zero. The rule below follows the cited construction (Blitz, Huij & Martens 2011) with one market factor. The three variations are unchanged.

**Score, at each month's first trading close T.**

1. **Market exposure.** Regress the stock's daily returns on SPY's daily returns over the **756 returns (36 months) ending at T−1**. This gives alpha and beta.
2. **Formation residuals.** For each day in the formation window, residual = stock return − (alpha + beta × SPY return).
   - v1.0 and v1.2: returns T−251 … T−21 (12-1 months, 231 days).
   - v1.1: T−125 … T−21 (6-1 months, 105 days).
3. **Score** = sum of the formation residuals ÷ their standard deviation (v1.2: the sum, not divided).
4. **History required.** At least 600 valid return pairs in the 36-month window and 80% of the formation days.
   - A stock without enough history is **unscorable** (not ranked). No substitute history is used.
   - This excludes stocks listed less than about 3 years; the bias is disclosed.

**Warm-up (owner approval 2026-09-29).**

- At the 2010 start the 36-month window reads 2007–2009 prices, as **signal warm-up/history only**.
- Those years are never used for parameter selection, hypothesis selection, performance evaluation or tuning.
- Universe eligibility and the IS evaluation begin on 2010-01-04.

**Terminology.** The *score* is market-adjusted: residual relative strength. The *portfolio* is long-only and **not beta-hedged**, so it is **not market-neutral**. It keeps market exposure.

**Portfolio.**

- Buy the top 10 by score, equal weight.
- Keep holdings that stay in the top 20 (a band, to cut turnover). Sell the rest.

**Daily refill.** A slot freed between rebalances (a sale, delisting or stale-exit) is refilled at the next close from the current month's ranking: the highest-ranked name not held. This avoids the month-long idle cash that the no-borrowing rule created for C01's monthly strategies.

**Positions:** 10. **Rebalance:** monthly, plus daily refill.

**Expected holding period:** about 2–6 months.

- With the monthly rank and keep band, about 3–4 names change a month (about 40–50 round trips a year): **1.4–1.7% of equity a year**. This is low cost.

## What result would falsify this idea?

- **Reject H008** if all three variations fail the IS screen.
- **Reject it also** if the best variation's beta to the equal-weight benchmark exceeds 0.9, or its max drawdown is not smaller than the benchmark's. The residual construction is claimed to remove the factor exposure; if it does not, the claim is false.
